Principal investigator

Fusheng Tang, Ph.D.

Principal Investigator · Associate Professor, UA Little Rock, Biology Department

Research interests: molecular biology of aging, intracellular sterol transport and cellular systems biology. Dr. Tang uses yeast as a model to study aging and the membrane-trafficking pathways that affect lifespan.

Dr. Tang received his B.S. in Biology from Central China Normal University (1984), his M.S. in Genetics from the Institute of Genetics, Academia Sinica, Beijing (1987), and his Ph.D. in Biochemistry from the University of Iowa (2002), followed by postdoctoral training in cell biology at the University of Iowa. He joined UA Little Rock as an Assistant Professor in 2005 and has been an Associate Professor since 2010. He has supervised more than 40 undergraduate researchers, many of whom have gone on to medical or graduate school.

Graduate students

Omotolani Jimoh

Ph.D. Candidate in Biology

Omotolani works on our first ongoing project, identifying lipids that remedy vacuolar membranes. She studies how up-regulation of Osh6 changes traffic from the trans-Golgi network to late endosomes and the vacuole, including in mutants of the retromer subunit Vps35, whose human counterpart is linked to Parkinson's disease. She built the strain collection that combines Osh6 overexpression with trafficking mutants, established the lab's CPY secretion western blot assay, and quantifies GFP-Snc1 trafficking by microscopy. She also mentors undergraduates in the molecular biology laboratory course.

Zakariya Tayyab

Ph.D. Candidate in Biology

Zakariya works on our second ongoing project, identifying lipids that allocate TORC1. He studies how blocking traffic from the trans-Golgi network to late endosomes affects autophagy and cell size in our Osh6 longevity mutant.

Former lab members

  • William King, Ph.D.

    Studied how up-regulated Osh6 enhances traffic from the trans-Golgi network to late endosomes and vacuoles, and how this differs between the mother cell and its bud (King et al., in preparation). He also studied the route of DNA uptake during yeast transformation.

  • Ilham Kadhim, Ph.D.

    Showed that up-regulation of Osh6 boosts an anti-aging membrane trafficking pathway toward vacuoles: it accumulates PI4P on the trans-Golgi network, and several of its effects depend on the adaptor protein Gga2 (Kadhim et al., 2022).

  • Nazneen Begum, Ph.D.

    Studied how up-regulation of Osh6 mimics calorie restriction (CR) for longevity and stimulates a unique form of endocytosis (Begum et al., in preparation).
