Rejuvenation of vacuoles in yeast cells

We use the budding yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae to study aging and how lipid transport and membrane traffic affect lifespan.

Explore our research

About the lab

The degradative organelle vacuoles start to age in generations 1 or 2 in a yeast cell that can replicate more than 20 generations. Although the bud inherits a fraction of vacuoles from an aged mother, it resets its age to 0. How the bud rejuvenates inherited vacuoles is an attractive theoretical question as well as an imperative pragmatic question, since aged lysosomes, the human counterpart of vacuoles, cause Parkinson’s disease and other age-dependent diseases.

In our studies, we found that the non-vesicular lipid transporter Osh6 declines with age. Intriguingly, a mild up-regulation of Osh6 remedies vacuolar morphology in mid-aged cells and extends the replicative lifespan of yeast cells by more than 30%. This Osh6 longevity mutant amplifies multiple asymmetric phenotypes (actin cables, endocytosis, protein localization, etc.) between the bud and its mother cell. Further explorations of this longevity mutant would reveal how the bud rejuvenates its inherited vacuoles.
