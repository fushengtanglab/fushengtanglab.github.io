Research

We combine yeast genetics, cell biology, and systems biology to understand how membrane traffic shapes aging.

Aging

Molecular biology of aging

Yeast cells age in ways that resemble human cells, making them a powerful model for identifying genes and pathways that control lifespan. Our work has linked lifespan extension to vacuole fusion and a form of autophagy (Tang et al., 2008) and used systems biology to identify longevity genes (Tan et al., 2009).

Lipid transport & membrane trafficking

Osh6 and traffic to the vacuole

Our earlier work showed that overexpressing the lipid-transfer protein Osh6 extends yeast lifespan by increasing vacuole fusion (Gebre et al., 2012) and boosts an anti-aging membrane trafficking pathway toward vacuoles (Kadhim et al., 2022). We now study which membrane-trafficking pathway carries Osh6's effect, as Osh6 transports the lipids PI4P and phosphatidylserine from the trans-Golgi network (TGN) to the vacuole.

Current direction

We combine Osh6 up-regulation with mutants that block different steps of traffic between the TGN, endosomes, and the vacuole, including gga2, ent3, vps9, gtr1, ypt7, and the retromer subunit vps35. Following the sorting of the vacuolar enzyme CPY, the secretory SNARE Snc1, and the plasma membrane ATPase Pma1, our results suggest that up-regulated Osh6 enhances traffic from the TGN to late endosomes, with different outcomes in the mother cell and its bud.

Because late endosomes and lysosomes decline with age, and endosome-derived secretion is involved in age-related diseases such as Parkinson's disease, our work may point to ways of keeping these organelles healthy during aging.

How we work

  • Yeast genetics: gene deletions, chromosomal promoter replacement (e.g., PERG6-OSH6) and combined mutant strains, confirmed by PCR.
  • Live-cell fluorescence microscopy: GFP-tagged proteins such as Snc1 and Pma1, FM4-64 to follow endocytosis and vacuoles, and actin staining.
  • Protein sorting and secretion assays: following the vacuolar enzyme carboxypeptidase Y (CPY) inside cells and in the growth medium.
  • Growth and stress assays: spot assays and calcofluor white sensitivity.
  • Aging and systems biology (earlier work): lifespan measurements and computational approaches to find longevity genes and model cellular processes.
