Microscopy services

In addition to our own research, our lab manages the campus fluorescence microscope, which is available to UA Little Rock faculty, staff, and students.

Fluorescence microscope

A Nikon widefield fluorescence microscope with a Plan Apo 100× oil-immersion objective (NA 1.4) and a Nikon DS-Qi2 high-sensitivity monochrome camera. It can take:

  • Brightfield and DIC images that show the outline and shape of cells, buds, and vacuoles.
  • Fluorescence images of green (e.g., GFP), red (e.g., mCherry, FM4-64), and blue/UV (e.g., DAPI, calcofluor white) labels.
  • Merged images that overlay fluorescence on DIC to show where a protein sits in the cell.
  • High-magnification images of live or fixed cells, such as yeast, bacteria, and cultured cells, detailed enough to see organelles such as vacuoles and endosomes.

Image analyses

Images are captured with Nikon NIS-Elements and saved with their scale and camera settings. With NIS-Elements and the free software Fiji/ImageJ, we can:

  • Build figures: merge channels, add scale bars, and assemble multi-panel figures.
  • Measure fluorescence: brightness of whole cells or selected regions, for example, plasma membrane versus cell interior, or bud versus mother cell.
  • Score and count: the proportion of cells with a given protein location or organelle shape, such as fragmented vacuoles.
  • Measure size: the area and length of cells and organelles.
  • Compare two colors: intensity profiles along a line and colocalization of two labeled proteins.
